How it works

From a first application to a stable future.

STEP works in three stages. Here’s what happens from the day someone applies to the day they move on to their next job.

1

Step 1: Start working

Getting in the door and earning from week one.

Apply

People experiencing homelessness apply to join the program, often after hearing about it from a friend or someone who’s already come through it. No résumé or spotless work history required—just a willingness to show up and try.

Onboard

The first month lays the groundwork for employment. Participants complete weekly training, check in regularly with our team, and start earning wages right away.

2

Step 2: Build skills and stability

Steady, paid work and the routine that comes with it.

Work

Participants take on consistent, paid work: 10 hours a week working alongside BYU economics students in the Record Linking Lab. Together they help connect every person recorded in a U.S. census since 1800 to the FamilySearch Family Tree: merging duplicate records, adding missing individuals, cross-referencing information across databases, and filling in missing details for people already on the tree.

Employment

After the first month, participants are hired as part-time employees. They keep building skills that carry well beyond this project: working with records and databases, using Excel and other everyday tools, professional writing, and keeping a consistent schedule.

Stabilize

Participants work remotely for a set period, earning steady pay. It’s a foothold that unlocks a lot: even a modest, consistent income helps secure housing, a working phone, hygiene items, and transportation.

3

Step 3: Move on to a lasting job

Ready for what comes after STEP.

Move forward

Our goal is not to keep participants in the program indefinitely, but to prepare them for opportunities beyond it. Through meaningful work experience, professional development, and assistance obtaining essential documentation, participants are equipped to pursue stable, higher-paying employment.

The work

What the Record Linking Lab does

The BYU Record Linking Lab, part of the Department of Economics, builds tools that link families and individuals across historical records. Everything it does is meant to improve the Family Tree at FamilySearch.org, a free public tree that aims to connect everyone in the world, so more people can find their families.

The lab’s largest effort is the Census Tree: linking the 217 million people who lived in the United States between 1850 and 1940 across every census they appear in, and connecting each person to their parents, siblings, spouses, and children. When it’s finished, it will be the largest long-term data set ever created in the U.S., opening up new research in economics, demography, sociology, and public health.

The lab pairs machine learning, which reads and matches handwritten records quickly, with careful work by people, which catches what the software misses, especially for groups that are hard to link, like women and immigrants. STEP participants do that careful human work alongside more than 50 BYU students.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Who can join the program?

The program is for people experiencing homelessness in Utah County. You don’t need a résumé or a recent job to apply.

Do participants need their own computer?

No. The work is fully remote and can be done anywhere with internet access, including public libraries, university campuses, shelters, or even a mobile device.

Are participants paid?

Yes. Participants are paid from the start of onboarding. After the first month, they are hired as part-time employees.

How long do people stay in the program?

Participants work for a set period, then move on. Along the way, our team helps them get ready for what comes next, from essential documents to a steadier job.

What does the work actually involve?

Alongside BYU economics students, participants help connect every person recorded in a U.S. census since 1800 to the Family Tree: merging duplicate records, adding missing individuals, cross-referencing information across databases, and filling in missing details for people already on the tree.

How can I refer someone?

Use our contact form and choose “Referring someone.” Caseworkers and shelter staff are welcome to reach out too.
