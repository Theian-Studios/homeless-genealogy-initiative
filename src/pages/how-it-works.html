How it works

Six steps from a first application to a stable future.

No resumé or spotless work history required, just a willingness to show up and try.

  1. Apply

    Unhoused participants apply to join the program, often after hearing about it from a friend or someone who’s already come through it. No resumé or spotless work history required — just a willingness to show up and try.

  2. Onboard

    The first month lays the groundwork for employment. Participants complete a weekly training, check in regularly with our team, and begin earning paid wages immediately.

  3. Work

    During this first month, participants take on consistent, paid work: 10 hours a week contributing to a genealogical research effort, all in tandem with a lab of students working through the BYU economics program. Each day, our participants and students alike help build the complete family tree of everyone born from 1800 onward. Their tasks include merging duplicate records, adding missing individuals, cross-referencing information across databases, and closing gaps for people already on the tree.

  4. Employment

    After their first month of onboarding, we legally employ participants as part-time employees. Our new employees continue to develop skills that carry well beyond this project: working with records and databases, using Excel and other everyday tools, professional writing, and creating a consistent schedule.

  5. Stabilize

    Participants work remotely for a set period, earning steady pay. It’s a small foothold that unlocks a lot: even a small but consistent income helps to secure housing, reliable phone access, hygiene items, and transportation.

  6. Move forward

    Our goal is not to keep participants in the program indefinitely, but to prepare them for opportunities beyond it. Through meaningful work experience, professional development, and assistance obtaining essential documentation, participants are equipped to pursue stable, higher-paying employment.

Skills participants take with them

  • Working with records and databases
  • Excel and other everyday tools
  • Professional writing
  • Keeping a consistent schedule
  • Recent work experience
  • Help obtaining essential documents

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Why can’t I give to one specific participant?

Every gift supports the whole program, so it pays wages and training for everyone in it. We share participants’ stories so you can see the people behind the work, not so they have to compete for support.

Who can join the program?

The program serves members of Utah County’s unhoused community. No résumé or spotless work history is required, just a willingness to show up and try. Many people hear about it from a friend or someone who has already come through it.

Do participants need their own computer?

No. The work is fully remote and can be done anywhere with internet access, including public libraries, university campuses, shelters, or even a mobile device.

Are participants paid?

Yes. Participants earn paid wages from the start of onboarding. After the first month, they are legally employed as part-time employees.

How long do people stay in the program?

Participants work for a set period. The goal is not to keep anyone in the program indefinitely, but to prepare them for stable, higher-paying employment beyond it.

What does the work actually involve?

Alongside BYU economics students, participants help build the family tree of everyone born from 1800 onward: merging duplicate records, adding missing individuals, cross-referencing information across databases, and closing gaps for people already on the tree.

How can I refer someone?

Use our contact form and choose “Referring Someone to the Program.” Caseworkers and shelter staff are welcome to reach out too.

Know someone who could use this job?

Get in touch about the employment program, or support the work with a donation.
