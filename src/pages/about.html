Putting Down Roots

The Homeless Genealogy Initiative is a transitional employment program. It offers paid, meaningful work to people experiencing homelessness in Utah County and helps preserve family connections for future generations.

Breaking the cycle

The barriers start before the first day of work.

Imagine receiving a job offer—but being unable to accept it because you don’t own a pair of work boots. Or because your identification was stolen. Or because you can’t afford the bus fare to get there. Perhaps you never receive a job offer at all because your résumé has a gap explained only by the label “homeless.”

For thousands of people experiencing homelessness, these aren’t hypothetical situations. They are everyday realities.

Employment is one of the surest paths out of homelessness, but finding and keeping a steady job is full of impossible contradictions. Employers expect:

  • References
  • Reliable transportation
  • Clean clothing
  • A clean record
  • Government-issued identification
  • A reliable phone connection
  • A permanent address

Without a job, these basic necessities are often out of reach.

Building opportunity

We meet people where they are.

The Homeless Genealogy Initiative (HGI) was founded to help break that cycle. Founded in 2026, we are a transitional employment program for people experiencing homelessness in Utah County. Employees contribute to a large-scale family history research project hosted by Brigham Young University’s Department of Economics. Our program provides temporary, flexible, remote employment where participants develop professional skills, receive on-the-job training, regain professional confidence, and access resources that prepare them for long-term employment.

We don’t expect our community members to have overcome all the barriers of homelessness before they are able to work. Instead, we acknowledge these challenges and have designed our employment model to meet them where they are.

Fully remote. For participants without reliable transportation, our fully remote work allows them to complete assignments anywhere they have internet access, including public libraries, university campuses, shelters, or even a mobile device.

10 hours a week. Many participants are balancing housing instability, medical appointments, or other personal challenges, so employees work only 10 hours a week on a schedule they set themselves. That provides flexibility while building consistency and responsibility.

Weekly training. Participants also receive weekly training that builds technological skills, strengthens workplace confidence, and helps them overcome employment gaps.

Work that matters

Connecting every person in the U.S. census since 1800 to the Family Tree.

The work itself matters, too. Employees collaborate with Brigham Young University’s Record Linking Lab, whose ambitious mission is to connect every individual recorded in the United States census since 1800 to the FamilySearch Family Tree.

Participants locate missing individuals, merge duplicate records, connect historical sources, and improve the accuracy and completeness of one of the world’s largest genealogical databases.

A lasting impact

Two goals that outlast the program.

First, we help participants secure long-term employment by building meaningful work experience, developing transferable skills, and overcoming the barriers that keep people out of work. Second, through their work with Brigham Young University’s Record Linking Lab, employees make a lasting contribution to historical and genealogical research by helping preserve family connections for future generations. In doing so, the program creates value both for the individuals it serves and for the communities and families who benefit from their work.
