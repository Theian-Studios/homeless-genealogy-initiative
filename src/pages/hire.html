What participants bring

Skills built on real work.

Every week, participants locate missing individuals, merge duplicate records, and cross-check sources to improve one of the world’s largest genealogical databases. That work builds skills that carry well beyond this project.

  • Working with records and databases
  • Excel and other everyday tools
  • Professional writing
  • Careful, detail-oriented research
  • Remote, self-directed work
  • Setting and keeping a consistent schedule

Look past the gap

A résumé gap isn’t the whole story.

For someone with real experience and a strong work ethic, the hardest part is often getting someone to give them a chance.

Experience you might not expect

Gregory, a participant, has been a construction foreman, a machinist, and a licensed tattoo artist. He has also worked in digital printing and run his own landscaping business and tattoo shop. Read his story.

Support toward stability

Participants earn steady pay, get weekly training, and receive help obtaining essential documentation, so they can pursue stable, higher-paying work.

Questions

Hiring FAQ

What kinds of roles are participants a good fit for?

It depends on the person. Participants come from very different backgrounds, from construction and machining to family history work. In the program, all of them build experience with remote work, records research, and everyday software. Tell us about your role and we’ll let you know if someone may be a fit.

How do we get started?

Send us a short message about the position using our contact form and choose “Hiring participants.” We’ll follow up with you.

Who runs the program?

BYU’s Department of Economics runs the program. Participants work with the university’s Record Linking Lab in Provo.
