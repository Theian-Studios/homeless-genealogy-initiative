What is Putting Down Roots?

The Homeless Genealogy Initiative

A transitional employment program that offers paid, meaningful work to members of Utah County’s unhoused community — and helps preserve family connections for generations to come.

Breaking the cycle

Imagine receiving a job offer — and being unable to accept it.

Imagine receiving a job offer—but being unable to accept it because you don’t own a pair of work boots. Or because your identification was stolen. Or because you can’t afford the bus fare to get there. Perhaps you never receive a job offer at all because your resume has a gap only explained by the label “homeless.”

For thousands of people experiencing homelessness, these aren’t hypothetical situations. They are everyday realities.

While employment is one of the surest paths out of homelessness, for this population, securing and keeping a steady job is full of impossible contradictions. Employers expect:

  • References
  • Reliable transportation
  • Clean clothing
  • A clean record
  • Government-issued ID
  • A reliable phone connection
  • A permanent address

Without a job, these basic necessities are often out of reach.

Building opportunity

We meet people where they are.

The Homeless Genealogy Initiative (HGI) was founded to help break that cycle. We are a transitional employment program which has served members of Utah County’s unhoused community since 2026. Employees contribute to a large-scale family history research project hosted by Brigham Young University’s Department of Economics. Our program provides temporary, flexible, remote employment where participants develop professional skills, receive on the job training, regain professional confidence, and access resources that prepare them for long-term employment.

We don’t expect our community members to have overcome all the barriers of homelessness before they are able to work. Instead, we acknowledge these challenges and have designed our employment model to meet them where they are.

Fully remote

For participants without reliable transportation, our fully remote work allows them to complete assignments anywhere they have internet access, including public libraries, university campuses, shelters, or even a mobile device.

Ten flexible hours a week

Because many participants are balancing housing instability, medical appointments, or other personal challenges, employees work only ten hours per week on a schedule they create themselves, providing flexibility while building consistency and responsibility.

Weekly training

Participants also receive weekly training that builds technological skills, strengthens workplace confidence, and helps them overcome employment gaps.

Work that matters

Connecting every person in the U.S. census since 1800 to the Family Tree.

The Homeless Genealogy Initiative also allows employees themselves to assist in a meaningful effort. Just as importantly, participants contribute to work with lasting significance. Employees collaborate with Brigham Young University’s Record Linking Lab, whose ambitious mission is to connect every individual recorded in the United States census since 1800 to the FamilySearch Family Tree.

Participants locate missing individuals, merge duplicate records, connect historical sources, and improve the accuracy and completeness of one of the world’s largest genealogical databases.

Locate missing individuals

Finding people who belong on the tree but haven’t been recorded yet.

Merge duplicate records

Bringing scattered records together so each person appears once, accurately.

Connect historical sources

Linking census entries and other sources to the people they describe.

A lasting impact

The Homeless Genealogy Initiative exists to achieve two lasting goals.

In doing so, the program creates value both for the individuals it serves and for the communities and families who benefit from their work.

First: long-term employment

We help participants secure long-term employment through building meaningful work experience, developing transferable skills, and overcoming employment barriers through employment.

Second: preserving family connections

Through their work with Brigham Young University’s Record Linking Lab, employees make a lasting contribution to historical and genealogical research by helping preserve family connections for future generations.
