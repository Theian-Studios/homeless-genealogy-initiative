For Jan, every person has a story worth knowing.

“This has been an opportunity to learn about people I might never have met otherwise,” she explains. Even over the past two months, while navigating homelessness in the cold Utah winter months, Jan never lost that spirit. Her bubbly personality and genuine love for others naturally draws people to her light.

That light is what attracted Brenda. After a family baptism, Brenda stopped at a nearby restaurant and said to Jan, “You don’t look like a normal homeless person. What’s your story?” After chatting for a while, Brenda and her husband Mark gave Jan enough money to restart her phone plan. “Ever since,” Jan says with an amazed smile, “it’s been one miracle after another, after another, after another, after another.”

> “It’s been one miracle after another, after another, after another.”

People often ask Jan, “What’s your story?” But that’s the same question she asks when doing family history work. For Jan, genealogy isn’t just about records or archives—it’s about people. Each document represents a sacred child of God with work that needs to be done in the temple.

Sponsoring Jan does more than provide temporary employment for someone getting back on their feet. It supports a woman whose faith led her to move to Utah, lose everything she had along the way, and still wake up every day with gratitude and hope.

As Jan puts it: “I know what I want to do, and I know the steps I need to take. I’m just so grateful to be here. This feels like it was by design. It’s going to be fun.”
