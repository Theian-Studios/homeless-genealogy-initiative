Jessie has been through more than most people would guess from meeting him.

His life has included addiction, homelessness, all types of abuse, family conflicts, a painful custody battle over his son, and a messy divorce that has lasted far longer than his marriage did. He describes years of difficult experiences almost matter-of-factly, occasionally stopping to acknowledge just how much it has been. “It is a lot,” he said. “That’s why I’m like, yeah, my plate’s 70% full right now.”

And yet, sitting across from Jessie, it is difficult to miss how determined he is not to let that 70% become the whole story. “I look at the glass half full of everything.” That attitude isn’t because Jessie has forgotten what happened. He knows exactly what he has been through, and he knows that he has survived it. “I beat addiction,” he said. “I beat this, I beat that.”

Part of his tenacity came through the 12 step program, when Jessie had a partial spiritual awakening in 2020. He said, “I was finally trying to take my life seriously.” But the years that followed brought more hardship, including losing a custody battle over his 5 year old son while trying to get him out of an unsafe household.

“I have every reason to use right now,” Jessie said. “I lost all my family. I lost my ex wife. I lost everything because they’re all going to prison. I am truly alone. And so I have every reason to throw my life away right now.” But, Jessie didn’t. Instead, after already going through the program once, Jessie kept moving through the 12 steps, this time on his own, and searching for the spiritual awakening he felt was still missing.

“I already made all my atonements and stuff, but like, where’s the spiritual awakening?” he asked. Eventually, he says, it came. After publicly coming out, Jessie describes suddenly feeling a profound shift. He had spent years trying to understand himself, and the different parts of his identity. He describes his mind as having two sides that used to fight for control, a defense mechanism from years of enduring childhood trauma.

“And then when I had finished my 12 steps and had my spiritual awakening, they learned to love each other.” He laughs at the strange simplicity of the way he now understands his healing process. But underneath the humor is something much more serious. Jessie says the spiritual awakening allowed him to finally understand his own mind and learn to accept himself. “When I had that spiritual awakening to really open up and let me learn about everything about my mind and learned how to love myself, and through that act of self love, how much healing came from it.” Then he puts the significance of that experience plainly: “If I did not learn that, I would not be standing here today.”

Jessie remembers the moment of his spiritual awakening vividly. He describes coming home after an intensely emotional experience and hearing what he understood to be Jesus asking him, *Are you ready to walk with me?* He was home alone. “And I just said yes.” He describes feeling something “drop into my body.”

> “And then my whole world lit up.”

His response was immediate: “I was like, this is what I’ve been missing… I found God and I found Jesus.” For Jessie, the connection between that experience and his recovery is clear. “Those 12 steps are very real.” His spiritual awakening gave him a reason to keep moving forward.

“I didn’t want to use anymore. I didn’t want this anymore. I just wanted to keep going and go.” The determination in that statement is the same determination Jessie brings to the rest of his life. He knows recovery doesn’t mean his problems disappear. He knows there are still difficult things ahead. But he has found something worth moving toward. Part of that is community. When Jessie talks about the program, he doesn’t describe it simply as a job or a way to make money. He talks about the people in it.

“Coming still to a place with others, the same goals in mind, you know, going through the same thing, it just builds, quote unquote, family, brotherhood, sisterhood, whatever you want to call it or label it in those regards.” Then he adds: “And very, very, very healthy and healing.” That sense of belonging has given Jessie something to build on. But perhaps even more striking is what he wants to give back. Early on, Jessie told Rachael that his goal in life was “to bring sunshine to everybody.”

He still means it. “I enjoy being a little ray of sunshine.” That choice seems especially meaningful coming from someone who has experienced so much hurt. Jessie understands that homelessness is not simply a matter of individual choices.

“The biggest thing I wish people understand of homelessness is it’s not always just that person,” he said. “It’s also there could be their circumstances surrounding themselves.” He knows what it feels like to have circumstances pile up faster than a person can handle them. He also knows how much a small amount of kindness can matter. “People don’t realize how hard homelessness and just how far a small bit of gratitude truly does go for one of us.”

Jessie’s story isn’t about pretending that everything that happened to him was okay, but unwaveringly moving forward. Jessie chose to keep going. He chose to find God. He chose recovery. He chose community. And, perhaps most importantly for Jessie, he chose to be a little bit of sunshine for someone else.

> “I enjoy being a little ray of sunshine.”
